The Notations in Chess

Algebraic notation is the universal language of chess. It allows players to record games, read chess books, and study tactics. Understanding it is the first step to improving your game. But first, we need to understand more about the Chessboard grid.

The Chessboard Grid

The chessboard is a grid made of 64 squares. Every square has its own unique two-character coordinate: a letter and a number.

  • Files (Columns): The eight vertical columns are labeled a through h, starting from White's left side.

  • Ranks (Rows): The eight horizontal rows are numbered 1 through 8, starting from White's side (Rank 1) and moving up to Black's side (Rank 8).

  • A Diagonal is a straight line of squares of the same color running slantwise across the board. The Bishop is the only piece that moves exclusively along diagonals.

Piece Abbreviations

Each chess piece is represented by a single capital letter. They are as follows :

  • K: King
  • Q: Queen
  • R: Rook
  • B: Bishop
  • N: Knight (used because 'K' is already taken by the King)

IMPORTANT NOTE : Pawns do not get a capital letter. When a pawn moves, you only write the name of the destination square.

How to Write Moves

A standard move is written by combining the piece letter with its destination square.

For example,

  • Nf3: Knight moves to the f3 square.
  • Be4: Bishop moves to the e4 square.
  • e4: A pawn moves to the e4 square (no piece letter).

Special Symbols

Chess notation uses specific shorthand symbols to describe events on the board :

  • Captures (x): When a piece captures an opponent's piece, insert an x between the piece letter and the destination square.
    • Example: Bxf7 means Bishop captures on f7.
    • Pawn captures: Use the starting file letter of the pawn. exd5 means the pawn on the e-file captured a piece on d5.
  • Check (+) : Placed at the very end of a move.
    • Example: Qh5+
  • Checkmate (#): Placed at the very end of a winning move.
    • Example: Qxf7#
  • Castling :
    • O-O represents Kingside castling (short).
    • O-O-O represents Queenside castling (long).
