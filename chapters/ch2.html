02 The Piece Family

Each piece has a unique style and movement pattern. These different pieces are known as the Pawn, Rook, Knight, Bishop, Queen, and King. Learning how each piece works gives you the foundation for strategy, tactics, and positional understanding.

Pieces: Pawn, Rook, Knight, Bishop, Queen, King
Fun fact: The square where they begin is called the home square.

The Pawn

The Pawn moves only forward, one square at a time, but captures diagonally. On its starting square it can move two spaces, which makes it powerful in opening play. It is also the only piece that cannot move backward.

Important Note: The Pawn is the only piece that cannot move backward, while all the other pieces can move in reverse as well.

The Rook

The Rook is the most straightforward piece on the board. It moves only in straight lines, vertically or horizontally, and can dominate open files and ranks. It is powerful in attack and can support major tactical ideas.

The Knight

The Knight is the most unusual piece because it moves in an L-shape. It is the only piece that can jump over other pieces, making it excellent for forks, surprise attacks, and tactical tricks. It often changes the rhythm of the game.

The Bishop

The Bishop moves diagonally in any direction for any number of squares. It is a long-range attacker and can be especially powerful when working with the other pieces. It also shines in strategic formations such as the fianchetto.

The Queen

The Queen is the most powerful piece on the board. It can move straight or diagonally in any direction, combining the abilities of a rook and bishop. She is dangerous in open positions, but should be developed carefully to avoid early attacks.

The King

The King is the most important piece, and the goal of the game is to protect it while threatening the opponent’s king. It moves one square in any direction and can also castle as part of a special defensive move. The king must always be protected.
